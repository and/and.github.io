You call a friend and it rings out. Later you find out they were driving. Or you're in a meeting, your phone buzzes on the table, and you cut the call and hope they don't worry.

Nobody did anything wrong. The caller just couldn't tell.

Halo is an app for Android and iPhone that fixes this. It lets your contacts know you're busy before they call.

Halo in 30 seconds

  • You set a status, like 🚗 Driving or 📅 In a Meeting. One tap in the app or on the home-screen widget.
  • It appears next to your name on their phone. If your friend has you saved as Jane Doe, they now see Jane Doe 🚗 Driving.
  • They see it wherever your name shows up: recent calls, the incoming call screen, and WhatsApp.
  • When you're free again, your name goes back to just Jane Doe.

Nobody has to open Halo to check. They were about to call you anyway, and now they can see you're on the road.

The statuses

  • 🟢 Available
  • 🚗 Driving
  • 📅 In a Meeting
  • 🌙 Do Not Disturb
  • 🏋️ At the Gym

You can set them yourself, or let Halo do it. Under Auto-set, turn on Driving, Calendar meetings or Low battery, and Halo changes your status when it notices one of those. Each one has its own switch.

Getting started

Halo only works between people who both have it, so setting up takes two people. It takes about a minute.

  1. Install Halo from the link at the end of this page.
  2. Sign in with your phone number. You get a code by SMS. There's no username, password or profile to fill in.
  3. Allow access to your contacts. Halo needs this to find the people you invite and to add their status to their name on your phone.
  4. Invite someone. Tap + Invite under Family or Work and pick them from your contacts. Halo opens your share sheet with a link, so you can send it by WhatsApp, SMS or anything else.
  5. They install Halo and tap Accept. The invite is waiting for them when they sign in. From then on, you each see the other's status.

If they tap Ignore, the invite is deleted and you aren't told.

Family and Work

Not everyone needs to know you're at the gym.

  • Family sees your full status: 🚗 Driving, 📅 In a Meeting, 🏋️ At the Gym and so on.
  • Work sees only 🔴 Busy. A colleague learns it's not a good time, and nothing more.

You can move someone from one group to the other at any time.

Common questions

Do the people I call need Halo too?

Yes. You see the status of people who use Halo and have accepted your invite, and they see yours. Everyone else in your contacts stays exactly as it is.

Does it work between Android and iPhone?

Yes. An iPhone user and an Android user can share statuses with each other.

Will it mess up my contacts?

No. Halo adds the status to the end of a contact's name and keeps the original name. When the person is available again, it puts the original name back. Only people you share statuses with are changed.

How fast does a status change show up?

Usually within a few seconds. On iPhone it can sometimes take longer, because iOS decides when background apps get to run.

What does Halo know about me?

As little as it can:

  • No names, no contact list. Names come from the contacts already on each phone. Your address book never leaves your phone.
  • Only your current status. Halo's server stores your status (like 🚗 Driving), when you set it, and a few app settings. Your status is filed under a scrambled code made from your phone number, not the number itself.
  • Only people you've accepted. Nobody else can read your status, including people whose invite is still pending.
  • No ads and no tracking.

You can delete your account and its data from the app at any time. The privacy policy has the details.
